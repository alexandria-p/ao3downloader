Bernike was nervous. She thought most people probably would be, at least a little bit. But it was worse for B, because she just wasn’t a party person. 

Honestly, she wasn’t much of a very social person, period: lots of books, lots of parking her butt in the window seat back home with tea and a project, and especially lots of sitting with her friends while each of them did their own thing independently. Parallel play, her friend Jeri called it. 

Or at least, that’s what Jeri had called it, back when she still talked to B regularly. Moving to Ottawa had been great for B academically—her degree program was top-notch—but none of B’s friends were here, and it was so hard to make new friends... Jeri was in California, studying computer science; Mai was in Vancouver, trying to make a break in acting; and Billie was back home, taking care of her chronically ill parents instead of going to school because all of Billie’s older siblings were shitheads who didn’t pull their weight.

Old news; didn’t matter now. What mattered now was that Bernike was an introverted, isolated geek being invited to hang out with a whole collection of (presumably) beautiful, socially successful women, and she just wasn’t sure she could handle that.

And she really didn’t want to embarrass her new boyfriend, especially since he was really sweet and really, really hot, but... This was not B’s scene. 

Like, at all. 

There were way too many very pretty people here, the women slim and blonde, the men tall and well-muscled. B grabbed Jesse’s arm like he would vanish if she didn’t and followed behind him as he was led through the house to the enormous back patio. She felt mousy and drab, and also angry about feeling mousy and drab. It wasn’t a good set of feelings.

But the statuesque woman with too-perfect wavy blonde hair who had greeted them at the door and led them through the house—she’d introduced herself as “Cassie,” and B was already dysmally aware she was going to forget half the names she heard tonight—deposited them right next to a positive smorgasbord of food, and the air smelled like summer: charcoal and meat, beer and water. There was a pond in the back yard, and B could see two athletic young men and a handful of kids playing some kind of game in the grass next to it. 

Even as nervous as she was, B could still feel the afternoon sun melting some of the tension out of her shoulders.

“Gunny!” a handsome black man called to them from next to the grill, and B had to blink all over again at just how damned big that grill was. That grill was bigger than her dad’s old herb garden. Between it and the landscaping and all the patio furniture and umbrellas and all, they must have spent more on this patio than she was going to pay in rent all year. “Hey, welcome to the team, rook! Who’s this?”

And, damn, B might be stuck in this hellish lifestyle, because she would put up with a lot worse than too many pretty people if it meant she got to see Jesse beam at her like this some more. 

“This is Bernike,” Jesse said—bragged, honestly. “My girlfriend.” B pulled herself more upright and waved, trying to pretend to be friendly and outgoing like everyone else here. 

“Hey, welcome,” the guy introduced himself, a little less effusively than he had for Jesse. “I’m Zane, call me Bood. Looks like you met my wife Cassie. I hope you came hungry!”

He gave them a whole run-down of the food situation, which was coded by meat type and platter shape; he had to stop halfway through and go back to the beginning, because the explanation was long enough that, before he got to the end, two more guys showed up and he had to tell them, too. It was a lot of meat.

The two new guys—B had already forgotten their names, she was terrible at this—grabbed plates, buffed Jesse on his arm, and ran down shouting towards the guys by the pond. B and Jesse made their own plates and then stood awkwardly by the spread, not sure what to do now. 

After a second, B tugged them a few feet to the side, so they weren’t blocking the way to the food.

Out of the corner of her eye, she could see a short, bearded, cheerful-looking man arrive carrying a couple of crates; he was directed over towards the drinks. Another man came out of the house behind him, but B had a hunch this one wasn’t newly arrived; he probably just came out of the bathroom. She wasn’t sure why she thought that... something about the way he was tucking his phone into his pocket, maybe?

She watched as the second guy bent for a beer from the cooler, then straightened, caught sight of them, and brightened. “Gunny!” he called, heading towards them with his beer. “Good! Who is this?” 

And, maybe it was the hint of an accent, maybe it was the charisma that was positively pouring off of the man, but B suddenly, incredulously, knew exactly who this guy was. 

“Cap!” Jesse was beaming, he was beaming, and looking at that face B knew she was so doomed. “Meet Bernike, my girlfriend! She is so smart, Cap, she is just crazy smart, you’re gonna love her!”

B blushed and reached out a hand. She was talking to a millionaire, a celebrity, and quite frankly a total hottie if you liked them broad and angular-faced—which, judging by how much she liked kissing Jesse, B definitely did. But even with all that, the part that made her blush was Jesse describing her as smart. 

She was smart; no arrogance, it was a simple fact. But she still glowed to hear Jesse say it.

Doomed. She was so doomed. 

The doomedest.

He tugged them across the patio towards one of the umbrella-shaded tables, where two men and a woman were lounging with drinks and mostly-empty plates. “Rage Box!” Jesse greeted from over B’s head. “And Hazy!”

These nicknames were going to be the death of her.

“Oh!” said B, now seeing why he had brought them over here. 

Doctor Lisa was a handsome woman, rather than pretty. She had a strong chin and a sensual mouth, dark hair in a chin-length Karen cut, wide shoulders, and large, high breasts. She was also older than a lot of the women there; most of them looked to be in their twenties, and early twenties, at that, but Doctor Lisa was in her thirties at least, and probably the back half of that decade. 

“What kind...?”

B was looking at Lisa’s face, because Rozanov had just introduced her as a doctor and that was interesting, so she saw what happened to the woman’s expression as soon as B started to ask what kind of a doctor she was: the head tilted down, the eyes widened and blinked, an innocent smile spread across those well-curved lips. 

“Oh,” Doctor Lisa lied, “I’m just a pediatrician.”

“Okay,” B said, not sure how to avoid calling her on that. She looked at the two guys uncertainly. Wyatt Hayes was probably a few years younger than Doctor Lisa, early thirties and with blond curls that made him look even younger. He had a fond impatience on his face, like this was not the first time he’d heard his wife lie about her job. The other one—Rage Box, apparently?—was older, with gray sneaking into his short dark hair, and didn’t seem to have noticed anything strange. 

“Bernike here is ‘pre-med,’” Rozanov told them like he was giving them all a present, and it seemed to work: Doctor Lisa sat up again, and the mask pulled away, leaving her an interesting woman again.

It was a lot less scary after that, sitting at a table with four other people, one of whom was Jesse, instead of out in the middle of the gathering of forty or so. B was able to relax and enjoy the admittedly excellent food; both the grilled items and the cold salads which had been set out as sides were delicious. She answered questions about her major and her schooling approximately as politely as Lisa was asking them until that conversation had led them down to a dead-end, at which point she cast around for something else and found it on Hazy’s chest. She pointed at his shirt: “I take it you’re a Blue Beetle fan?”

Rage Box burst out laughing and Lisa sat forward, watching. 

"Hazy’s a big fan of comics in general,” Jesse explained, and considering he’d only known these people a few weeks that was probably an understatement. 

“Okay,” B said, and decided to let a little of herself show through. She turned and blinked up at Hazy with faux innocence. “But why would you go with the shittier Blue Beetle, and not Jaime Reyes?”

“Oooooh!” cooed Lisa, grinning at the oncoming conflict. “Are you trying to pick a fight?” She asked it like she was highly in favor of that idea.

Hazy just waved a chicken wing at her, though. “No, no, I like Ted Kord because I’m a big fan of the Beetle/Booster team-up, but Jaime Reyes has a lot of good things going for him! She might have a good argument!”

The topic carried them until Cassie and a couple of other women brought out a cascade of desserts, placing them on the now-partially-cleared buffet table with the rest of the food. There was a clink of silver on glass and a good-looking older man (“Wiebe,” Jesse whispered to her) gave a brief speech, welcoming everyone to the coming year of “damned good Centaurs hockey!” (No one called him on the oxymoron.)

Then the tunes were pumped up again and yet another gorgeous young woman threw herself down on the bench seat beside B with a wine bottle. “Almost done,” she said, pouring into Lisa’s glass without asking, then into her own rather more heavily, and then, with a mimed wiggle of the bottle to ask first, into B’s, too. Then she frowned. “Wait, who are you?”

“Damn, Nic,” Lisa laughed. “How much have you had?”

“I have a night off with no driving and as soon as we get home my mother-in-law will be taking the twins,” Nic said with a grin. “So, not as much as I’m planning to, actually!” Despite however much she’d had, Nic turned and was able to sink the wine bottle into the wide-mouthed trash can twelve feet away. “Two points!”

“From this distance? Three.” Jesse sounded impressed. 

“Thanks! Seriously, though, who are you kids? I don’t remember you from the last one of these.”

“They weren’t at the last one of these.” Lisa sipped her wine and looked at Nic with transparent fondness. “This is our rookie defenseman, Jesse Gunn, and his girlfriend Bernike—who seems to prefer being called ‘B.’ You two, this is Nicole Boyle. She’s a disaster at the best of times, but I would strangle a toddler to get an ass like that.”

“Slightly concerning coming from a pediatrician,” Rage Box observed equably. 

“No, no, that means I’ve seen too many of them,” Lisa explained. “Toddlers, not asses. They’ve lost all their charms!”

Nicole snorted. “Doctor or no doctor,” she told them, “Lisa is the only WAG not allowed to babysit.” 

“Child-free and proud, baby!” 

Hazy cheers’ed them with a bottle of the cider half the group was drinking, but B had a different question: “What’s a WAG?”

She found out in short order. By the time they left for the evening, the sun catching the dust on the inside of the windshield as Jesse drove one-handed in his too-loud car, Bernike had given her number to Lisa, Nicole and Cassie, and the last of these had added B’s number to a group chat that had her phone buzzing cheerfully all the way back to campus. 

That first night it was too overwhelming, texts and gifs coming in one after another for hours. Combined with too much stimulation at the party (and too much nervousness before it), B couldn’t handle even looking at it before the next morning. She set her phone on “do not disturb” and crawled into bed.

The next morning was Sunday, though, and B was able to lounge under the covers with Jesse’s arm thrown over her as the early-morning sunshine came through the window at an angle that snuck it past the shade.

Peter, her own lab bench partner, looked up at her with wide, startled eyes behind thick plastic-framed glasses. He didn’t say anything, but that wasn’t surprising, since Peter had said about seven total sentences to her all semester.

“Dallas Kent is not a chud!” Jean said angrily. “What do you know about hockey, eh, Bernice?”

“No, he is a chud,” corrected Justin, laughing, “but I do think it’s going a bit far to say we all knew it.”

“Well, you should have,” B snorted, like she’d known who Dallas Kent even was more than a month ago. “He wasn’t subtle.” She had at least known that everyone on the Cents hated Toronto, where Kent played, basically from the first moment she’d asked about Toronto—so, long before all this. That meant it had to be common knowledge, right?

“Name one sign,” Jean said. “You are always so know-it-all, acting so much smarter than everyone else, but this you could not have known! Name one sign.” The way he said it, B was starting to think her hatred here was not one-sided.

Justin looked between them, then turned to her, eyebrows raised.

And B wasn’t a dancing bear, so she didn’t have to tell Jean shit, but Justin maybe had one or two redeeming qualities, so she directed her voice to him when she said, “Dallas Kent starts more fights than just about anyone else in the division. No, he's not the one dropping gloves, but that's just because he's a rat who dips out and leaves it to his enforcers to handle. But it's him starting them, and he's not funny about it, he's just mean. Everyone knows it, even the refs."

Okay, so maybe B had looked it up after Janie mentioned it to Niko the other night.

“He’s also just got a terrible reputation with other teams; I know the Centaurs all kind of had the attitude that, ‘of course it was Dallas fucking Kent,’ when the news broke over the weekend.”

Jean scoffed. “That doesn’t make him guilty.”

“No, the guilt makes him guilty,” B shot back, just as scornful. It was kind of liberating, hating Jean out loud. At last.

“It does kind of prove the chud-ness, though,” Justin said slowly.

Jean scoffed again, now sounding genuinely angry. “She does not know hockey!” He turned to B and demanded, “Since when do you follow hockey, Bernice?”

He was so much easier to deal with once he’d lost his cool. Now she could just treat him like Damien, or Niko on his bad days: like a kid who needed parenting. She let her eyebrows climb up her head, tried to arrange her mouth like she did when Damien threw a tantrum in public. “Since I started dating a player.”

She left the word dumbass silent on the end, but they all heard it anyway.

“You are?” Justin again, getting in before Jean could say anything. Which was good, because Jean looked like he was gearing up to scoff harder than he had ever scoffed before in his life. “Wait, which team?”

“Uhhh, the Centaurs?” Did they think she was dating someone out in Montreal?

“For real?”

They all turned to look at Peter, who looked as surprised as any of them to have blurted that out. He flushed and ducked his head. “I just meant—you’re in school.”

“Yeah...” B said slowly. “So was he, until this summer. It’s his first year.”

“Which player?” Jean challenged.

Wait, what was happening? Why did this feel weird?

“Jesse Gunn,” B said, trying to work out why the conversation had shifted. “He’s a defenseman, usually on the second line.” Now, anyway; Wiebe had switched the defensemen up last month.

Justin and Jean looked at each other. Jean looked a lot calmer than he had a minute ago, his usual faint aloofness returning to him. “He’s having a decent year, isn’t he?”

“Think so, yeah,” said Justin. “Likely to be second line on the regular, if he keeps playing like he is.”

Jean turned back to B. “Well,” he said, with a weirdly friendly smile, “I guess we won’t be seeing you for much longer, then.”

Beside her, Peter made a sad sound of agreement. Justin’s face had closed off.

B did a double-take at all three of them. “What does that mean?”

“Don’t worry about it,” Jean said, smirking.

Justin gave B a weird look—sad? Apologetic?—then obviously directed himself to refocus on his laptop. “So, what order are we dropping these things in?”

And as much as she wanted to chase that bullshit down, they did only have so long to run this absolutely make-work experiment. B turned back to her own bench.

She watched the video (“TORONTO BROMANCE DEAD FOR GOOD?”) once she got out of lab. It had clearly been recorded on a cell phone at practice, which if the Guardians were anything like the Cents, they were not allowed to do. On the other hand, it looked like it had been recorded by someone off the ice, so probably not a player; she’d bet some Toronto staffer was about to be let go over this.

Troy Barrett was a lot prettier than B had anticipated, prettier frankly than she was comfortable with; give her Jesse’s wide-mouthed neanderthal-like ruggedness over high cheekbones and pouting lips, any day of the week. Apart from that, though, Barrett didn’t look good: too pale, except for spots of high color in his cheeks, probably from anger; dark circles under his eyes, and lips chapping. His hands were shaking, too, she noticed; anger? Or some kind of withdrawal? She knew the league tested, but there were things that would slip under the radar, including alcohol...

He and Kent weren’t wearing helmets in the video, and the person filming had gotten a good angle, so she could see every expression on Barrett’s face. There was no doubt in B’s mind: Barrett believed the accusations, and not in a “that’s probably true” way. He knew it was true.

And, according to the people in the comments, that was coming from Dallas Kent's best friend. Did he know before?, they all asked. They spent so much time together, how could this possibly be a surprise?

B thought it was a surprise, though. No way to know for certain, of course, but reading Barrett’s face now... B remembered the look on her friend Jeri’s face, when Billie had said she wouldn’t follow Jeri to school like they planned. It was the same look, really.

Troy Barrett was yelling at his friend because Dallas Kent had just, on some level, broken his heart.

Poor guy. B wondered if he knew it.
