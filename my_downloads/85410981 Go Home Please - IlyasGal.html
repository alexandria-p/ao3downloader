Chapter 1: Exile

Go home, please.

 

Go home, please.

 

Go home, please.

 

No other words were cycling through Shane’s head. 

 

Ilya had really told him to leave. 

 

Ilya, the person Shane loved more than anyone and who seemed to love him in return, had told him to leave. Shane was pretty sure that this hadn't happened since Sochi all those years ago.

 

The sick, twisted feeling in Shane's stomach was the same, if not worse.

 

He took a deep breath to try to calm the wave of nausea and anxiety bubbling up.

 

Blinking, he realized that he was just standing out in the December cold, facing his Jeep in the driveway, zoned out. He wasn’t sure what he was supposed to do next.

 

Another deep breath, and then he chose to turn, hopefully, and look back at the door to the Ottawa house. 

 

Ilya's house. Not my home. Go home, please, Shane thought. 

 

A small part of him thought that Ilya would change his mind and come after him. If Ilya did not welcome him back in and salvage the night, he could bring Shane back to keep fighting. Just for Ilya to keep him, rather than push him out into the cold.

 

Shane made himself look at the door.

 

Nothing had changed.

 

Ilya was not standing in the doorway. 

 

Nor was he looking at him from the window.

 

The door was still closed.

 

Shane had closed and locked the door himself when Ilya hadn’t moved to walk with him. Watching Ilya looking both furious and broken was crushing. He had put that look on Ilya's face, and he would follow the clear instruction Ilya gave him—it was the least he could do.

 

Go home, please.

 

As Shane made his way to the door, he realized this goodbye would be very different from any since they became exclusive. There would be no searing kiss pushed up against the door frame, the two of them hating that they would be ripped apart as soon as they opened the door. 

 

No, this time Shane had walked out by himself, no satiated Ilya staying in bed, no I love you, just contempt and silence, as he closed the door, quietly, and dutifully locked it out of habit.

 

We didn’t say I love you, Shane thought. It was worse than Vegas when they didn’t even kiss.

 

A shiver ripped through him. It was bitterly cold, and he had left his coat unzipped. He didn't care enough to fix it.

 

How long have I been standing here? That thought ripped through the echo of Ilya's words, which had been running through his head on a loop, if only for a moment.

 

Go home, please.

 

Not only had he not zipped his coat, he hadn’t engaged the remote start to warm the engine of the Jeep—like he always did in the winter.

 

In fact, he seemed to be on autopilot so much that he only realized he hadn't turned the remote start on when he started the car. He should have sat in the driveway and let the car warm up before pulling away.

 

But Ilya wanted him gone.

 

Go home, please, replayed in his head on loop. Ilya's voice had sounded …Shane wasn't even sure what it sounded like. It was some mix of furious, devastated, resolute, and worst of all, tired.

 

It was the "please" that killed Shane most of all. Ilya knew he wouldn't leave. It wasn't begging. It was a plea. It said, "If you don't go, I'll break." There were one hundred different ways Ilya could have kicked him out. He had chosen kindness. A harsh kindness. The "please" was a warning that things might not stay so civil if Shane pushed back. It was also a knife to Shane's gut.

 

Go home, please.

 

Shane started backing the car out of the driveway when the loud warning sound and the light on the dashboard started flashing—he hadn't fastened his seat belt. 

 

He buckled himself in and finished backing out of the driveway and away from Ilya.

 

On autopilot, Shane headed toward Montreal.

 

There was no podcast or audiobook playing, just the soundtrack of their fight looping over and over and over in his head.

 

Go home.

 

Please. 

 

Go home.

 

Please.

 

Go home.

 

Then, because anxiety is horrible and thoughts don’t stop, he wondered why he could not answer Ilya. “You wouldn’t even choose me, would you? If it is between me and hockey.” Why deflect the question back on Ilya when the answer was obvious? Ilya chose Shane. Shane knew that. He chose Ilya. He had chosen Ilya over Rose, in a way. Or rather, he chose to be truthful and then tried to get Ilya back. 

 

Before he knew it, Shane was pulling up to his condo in Montreal.

 

How had an over two-hour drive passed? He wondered.

 

Time flies when you are mentally flaying yourself, he thought bitterly.

 

He looked up at the building as he waited to turn into the underground parking lot.

 

Abruptly, he shut his blinker off. He could not make himself turn.

 

You are Home. Go inside, he thought as he kept circling the block, unable to make the turn into the underground garage. He just couldn't make himself do it.

 

Home. Home, as Ilya meant it, was Montreal, where Shane's apartment was. Where the Metros played. He’d spent the vast majority of his adult life in Montreal, but it wasn’t home. It was where he lived. Home was Ilya.

 

The thought was too much for Shane; he could feel a few hot tears escape down his cheeks, and he made an impulsive decision: to drive back to Ottawa. It was still early, he wasn't sure when he had left Ilya's, but it was only 3:30 now. He could be back at Ilya’s door for dinner.

 

Home.

 

Ilya said go home.

 

Shane was just following Ilya's own directive, he convinced himself.

 

The knot in his chest loosened a little.

 

Reality started settling in about thirty minutes into his drive back to Ottawa..

 

Ilya wanted him gone.

 

Ilya wanted space.

 

Shane hadn't answered the question.

 

Once again, he felt his eyes welling with tears.

 

He managed to get himself in hand and not cry. He knew how to hide signs of distress and keep a controlled facade. It did nothing to quiet his thoughts or increase his concentration.

 

Sometime afterward, he knew he shouldn’t be driving like this. He decided to pull off the highway if only for a few minutes. He pulled off the road at the next exit: Vankleek Hill, Ontario.

 

Halfway between Ottawa and Montreal, he thought humorlessly.

 

He stopped at a gas station to refuel and collect himself for a bit before he got back on the highway. He had been in the car for almost four hours.

 

He couldn’t drive back to Montreal again. That felt like admitting defeat.

 

I should just drive all the way back to Ottawa, he decided.

 

Just drive back to Ilya's and deal with this. Ilya would have had time to cool off, and he would listen this time. He would understand why Shane couldn't go to his team party. And then everything would be fine. Ilya would understand. 

 

The thought of the party sent a chill through Shane as if a cold bucket of water had been thrown on him.

 

Would Ilya even be home?

 

Had he gone to the party? It would be too early. Was he getting ready for the party?

 

Shane knew if he went back, he wouldn’t dare use his key. He would knock, giving Ilya the choice about letting him in. That was the thing—for once, Ilya didn't want Shane in his home. Shane wasn't welcome there. Not tonight, maybe not ever.

 

The party was out of the question. It was a ridiculous thing to ask. Shane couldn't give that to Ilya, especially not on a whim. However, he could give in to this other request to leave. He would respect Ilya's wishes.

 

He paid at the pump and got back in the car. 

 

Shane felt unmoored and uncertain; he started driving without any intention of getting back on the highway in either direction.

 

It seemed fitting to be stuck halfway between Montreal and Ottawa. Hockey and Ilya. It was like the half-life he had been living. If he went back to Montreal, then everything Ilya said was right. 

 

Hell, everything everyone said about me only caring about hockey was right, he thought. 

 

Shane couldn’t live with that thought, in the same way he could not go back to Montreal. However, he couldn’t say it didn’t matter. He was unable to throw caution to the wind, and attend the team party with Ilya.

 

He pulled into the parking lot of a Tim Horton’s and thought about going through the drive-through for a steeped tea. Quickly, he dismissed the idea because halfway between his hometown and where he played hockey, he would definitely be noticed. He didn't need questions about why a clearly upset-looking Shane Hollander was in Vankleek Hill on Boxing Day. Or worse, photos online.

 

He sat in his car on the furthest edge of the parking lot, trying to summon the courage to go back to Montreal and his empty condo.

 

The image of his building loomed large in his mind. It felt imposing, glass, modern, sterile, and wrong.

 

Ilya had once asked Shane if he was homeless when Shane hadn’t thought about bringing him to his apartment for a hook-up, but in a way, Shane was indeed without a home. His condo in Montreal was never his home. His home was his parents' house, and then, when it was built, his cottage. Now, home was wherever Ilya was, and Ilya didn’t want Shane in his home. 

 

He couldn't even go to the "hook-up/murder building" as Ilya referred to it; he sold that and put the proceeds toward the Irina Foundation.

 

Yeah, there is no way I'm going back to my empty condo, Shane decided.

 

It was empty in so many ways. Empty because it was impersonal. There was so little of him there. He couldn't even put up photos of him and Ilya there. He was afraid someone would come over at the last minute, see, and start asking questions.

 

Pictures. 

 

Ilya had given him the picture of them from the CCM photoshoot.

 

He had noticed the way Ilya had momentarily bristled when he said he would put it up at the cottage. It hadn't been a dig. It was just practical. It was the right move, but that didn't mean it didn't hurt Ilya. Ilya, who did something so thoughtful. Tracking down the photographer and getting the print. It made Shane feel a little bad that his gift wasn't more sentimental, even though Ilya had seemed to really like it.

 

He wondered if this made him a bad boyfriend.

 

No, a hundred other things do that, he thought bitterly.

 

Shane wished there was someone to help him because he felt like his own skin was crawling, his mind was racing, and nothing made sense. He knew he was in the middle of some sort of panic spiral, if not a full-blown panic attack, in the middle of the Tim Horton’s parking lot in Vankleek Hill.

 

He tried to think about anything else, like Buffalo's terrible power play, but nothing worked.

 

The thought dawned on him that maybe he should call someone for help. There was someone in his life who would tell him the right steps, the right thing to do, that would make this better or just make him feel better.

 

They had a plan. It was Ilya who wanted to deviate from it. Ilya was asking for more. Shane wasn't sure that was a bad thing, even if it was inconvenient and irresponsible.

 

Mentally, he went over the list. 

 

He couldn't call Ilya. All he wanted was for Ilya to tell him everything would be ok. But Ilya didn't want to speak to him. He sent him away.

 

Go home. Please.

 

He couldn't call Hayden. Hayden would probably gloat or attempt to hook him up with somebody. He thought about talking to Jackie, but Hayden was getting her friends’ phone numbers from somewhere, even if Jackie wasn't fully participating. Hayden didn't understand that Ilya was it for Shane. Talking to his supposed best friend would only make things worse.

 

Rose was next on his mental favourites list. She was too good a friend to refrain from pointing out his own part in the fight. Shane knew he was more responsible for the fight, but he could not handle hearing the truth. Not yet.

 

Next were his parents, but he just couldn't. His mom would be the person to call. She would give him objectivity, but she would also make him feel better. She would remind him that Ilya loved him (true), Ilya made his decision willingly (true), and that Ilya would do anything for Shane (also true). But right now, when he had hurt Ilya so much, he didn't want to be made to feel better. It was probably wrong, but sitting in this hurt felt right. Like he had to think about what he had done.

 

He needed to think things through on his own.

 

He needed to figure it out because there was one thing he knew: he wasn’t going to let their relationship fall apart.

 

But what if it already had?

 

What if he was happy in the little bubble they operated in and had missed the signs that things were falling apart? That what they had wasn't enough or that he wasn't enough for Ilya.

 

It wasn’t necessarily a conscious decision to start driving again, but he did start driving around. Not to Ottawa or Montreal, he just needed to move. 

 

He saw a modest chain hotel in the distance. As soon as he saw it, his decision was made. He got a room, so he wouldn't be far when Ilya cooled off and either called or texted.

 

Go home, please.

 

Go home, please.

 

When he parked, he checked his phone to see if, in his fog, he had missed a text or call.

 

He hadn’t. 

 

Ilya hadn't reached out.

 

Against his better judgment, he gave up what he thought was the last shred of dignity he had at that moment and sent one text to Ilya.

 

Jane: Please, let me come home. I'm sorry, Lyubimyy.

 

Shane didn't use Russian terms often, though he was trying to learn. Maybe it was playing dirty, but he was willing to do what was needed to open the lines of communication between them.

 

After sitting in the hotel parking lot for an undefined amount of time, probably fifteen to twenty minutes,  there was still no reply from Ilya. Ilya had not only not replied, but he had not even read the text. He gave up and went in to book a room. 

 

He was grateful the message had gone through, which meant he wasn't blocked. He had listened to teammates complain that their girlfriends had blocked them during fights when they needed a break.

 

Checking Ilya's location to see if he went to the party or was at home crossed his mind. However, they shared locations mainly because of the two-plus-hour drive in case a car broke down or there was some other emergency. What started out as a small act of caring and checking in now felt invasive. Shane had no right to Ilya's location.

 

Running through his mind was a continuous litany of, Go home, please.

 

Not wanting to look out of place, Shane pulled out the emergency bag he kept in the back of the Jeep. Though it had toiletries, clothes, and sundry items, it was also his car emergency kit with a blanket, flashlight, water, and protein bars. He never used it, as he usually stole Ilya's clothes and vice versa. Now, sadly, it was coming in handy.

 

By some mercy, even though it was around dinner time, the lobby was empty, and the clerk either didn’t care who Shane Hollander was or saw him having a rough time and let it go without comment when he booked the room.

 

He had never been to this hotel, but somehow it looked like every hotel he’d ever been in. When he entered his room, it was like a montage flashed before his eyes of him and Ilya hooking up in hotels. 

 

The tightness in his chest was getting worse. He felt like he could barely breathe at this point. He tried to take deep breaths.  He even splashed cold water on his face to try to shock his system.

 

It felt like he was shaking or vibrating, but when he held out his hand, it looked shockingly steady. 

 

Panic attack, and it isn't abating, he thought.

 

He didn’t focus on anything else but his box breathing.

 

Breathe in for four seconds- feel your lungs filling.

 

Hold for four seconds- do not breathe out.

 

Exhale through your mouth for four seconds until your lungs are empty.

 

Hold for four seconds- do not breathe in or out.

 

He repeated this process over and over and over again, changing the time of the interval.

 

Eventually, he got his breathing sort of under control.

 

He pulled out his phone, it was after nine pm, to check again to see if Ilya had texted.

 

There was one new text message.

 

Hope blossomed cautiously in Shane's chest. Maybe Ilya would let him come back. Maybe he didn’t care if they yelled and screamed all night, even though it wasn’t necessarily like them, but whatever it takes to fix this.

 

The message is not attached to Ilya's alias Lily; it is from JJ.



JJ: Capitaine this is my friend Eliott. 

JJ: Picture of JJ and Eliott 

JJ: I'm at a Christmas party with him. He's a physiotherapist. Got his number for you. You should message him or better yet, come meet up with us on New Year’s after the game.

JJ: Eliott's Contact with his name and number.

 

This was a simple thing that JJ had done more than a few times, but tonight it felt like an actual affront to Shane. 

 

Why, if the team knew he was out, was he not telling JJ to stop trying to set him up beyond just saying he wasn’t interested? Why wasn’t he shutting this down completely? 

Quickly, he typed: I have a boyfriend. Then he deleted it. 

 

A boyfriend meant things were serious.

 

A boyfriend was someone you introduced to your friends and teammates. 

 

A boyfriend would come to a game the way Rose had. 

 

While he would love to introduce Ilya as his boyfriend to JJ, he knew that wasn’t feasible.

 

He had to say something. He knew he couldn’t leave it like this because he couldn’t take it anymore. Couldn’t take trying to dodge a stupid hook-up that was undermining his relationship. He had to start putting them first. The way Ilya always did.

 

Why did I not answer the question? Because I’ve been putting hockey first. Hockey has to come first. It’s always come first. But something has to change. 

 

He couldn't change much, but there were things he could change.

 

Quickly, he settled on texting: I’m seeing someone. Please stop trying to hook me up with every random man you come across who happens to be gay.

 

JJ: Is it serious?

 

It was, Shane thought bitterly.

 

Shane didn’t want to answer any more questions, so he came up with something that he thought would make JJ back off.

 

Shane: I hope it will be. But seriously, please stop. Just because I keep my shit private doesn't mean I'm alone. But thanks for thinking of me, buddy. 

 

He could hear Ilya mocking him, "So polite Hollander, always so polite."

 

That brought a whole fresh wave of tears. Shane tossed his phone to the side and lay on the bed in his street clothes, replaying everything that happened.

Go home. Please, Ilya said.

 

It was the 'please' that broke Shane. 

 

Ilya saw his boyfriend’s shoulders slump, saw the fight drained out of Shane. Then he turned and walked out the door. 

 

Ilya wanted to run after him. Tell him he’s sorry, stupid, and that Shane should come back. A heady mix of ego, wounded pride, and anger kept his feet rooted to the floor, unable to follow Shane. Glued to his spot, he heard the key in the lock as Shane closed up the house on his way out. The sound of the lock broke the uneasy silence Ilya had caused.

 

He chuckled bitterly to himself: even in the midst of this fight, break up, whatever the fuck this was, of course, Shane would lock the door. He could be so damn considerate and maddening at the same time.

 

Shane wonders if I would choose hockey over him? The question made Ilya feel a blind rage come upon him. He knew if Shane didn't leave, he would say ugly things. The part of him he fought against, the part that was his father, might come untethered. He would never want to speak to Shane that way.

 

Sending him away. Getting some space and breathing room might feel horrible, but it had been the best thing to do.

 

Without much thought, he found himself pulling out a bottle of his favourite vodka. He looked at his watch and realized it was just past one in the afternoon.

 

It was 8 pm in Moscow. He smirked and poured four fingers into a glass. Being angry and holding a glass of vodka made him think of Vegas.

 

Over the last three years, he and Shane had spoken about it, and Ilya now knew that Shane was angry that they didn't kiss. Ilya had just been angry and used Shane. It was hot. But he had some guilt about it.

 

He had some guilt about tonight.

 

Once again, they didn't kiss. More importantly, they hadn't said I love you when Shane left.

 

It left Ilya feeling uneasy and incomplete. 

 

The vodka soothed his frayed nerves, but it didn't make him feel better.

 

Why couldn't he just leave well enough alone? He had more happiness than he had experienced since his mother died, and yet he was selfish and wanted more. Maybe not more, but to feel equal.

 

Who could he tell about Shane?

 

He didn't have parents to tell, to win over—not that his father would have ever been okay with Shane in any way, shape, or form. His mother would have loved him, if only because of how he loved Ilya.

 

Loved. 

 

Hopefully that wasn't past tense.

 

His brother was a waste of space and breath. He would sooner turn them into the police, or blackmail them for money than welcome Shane into their fucked up family. Shane would never know his niece, a sweet girl who probably didn't remember Ilya or that she even had a dyadya. 

 

Erased. Ilya was erased from his family, or rather, removed himself after his father died.

 

Sveta and Cliff, he didn't speak to either of them very often since moving to Ottawa. They had their own lives in Boston. He thought they might even be a little mad at him for leaving without letting them know beforehand and without a real reason. They were as in the dark as the rest of the hockey world.

 

Sveta would have no problem. He was fairly sure she suspected Jane was Shane, but at the very least, she knew that his Jane was a man. He thought that Shane might still be jealous of Sveta—stupid man, thought Ilya would just have a "normal life" with her. As if Shane loving him hadn't fundamentally shifted his entire existence.

 

Cliff, he knew, would be okay. He'd never treat Shane the way Pike treated Ilya. But he wasn't so sure how good he would be with the secret of it all.

 

Then there was his team. These nice, inclusive people, who he felt would keep his secret. This was such a big part of him. Even if he didn't tell them about Shane, he wanted them to know he was bisexual. He knew people might assume he was a homophobe because... Russia, and just wanted people to know that he was okay, and he was one of them.

 

He put the bottle back and brought his drink with him to the abandoned living room—the scene of the crime.

 

He sat there for a while, sipping his drink.

 

Just the one, he told himself, though he knew it was probably a lie.

 

He allowed himself to get lost in the fantasy of going to the party with Shane, if he hadn’t blown up the conversation by voicing his doubts about where Shane’s loyalties lie.

 

After Shane had said no, Ilya would have explained better, "If Cliff were in town or Sveta, I would bring them to party. Bood says bring people. You are just friend alone tonight. So sad. So you tag along. No big deal. People know we are friends—we run charity together."

 

"Okay, you’re right,” Shane would’ve acquiesced. “We tell them I'm in town visiting my parents--"

 

"I wanted to liven up boring night. For you, I'm good friend."

 

Shane would have rolled his eyes and called him an asshole.

 

Ilya would have fucked Shane in the shower to get him to stop worrying about the party. Shane would have stressed about what to wear, but refused to wear something of Ilya's, the way he would have if they were just going to his parents' house.

 

They would have gone to the party in separate cars, Shane arriving twenty to thirty minutes after Ilya. 

 

Sure, they would have to answer questions about the rivalry being fake, but they played hard against each other despite being friendly. Ilya would have loved to see Luca freak out about talking to the Shane Hollander. Shane would finally get to know the nice people who were so accepting of Ilya. 

 

Shane would have stayed an hour or two before he left. Ilya would stay another hour after that. Then they would have met back up at Ilya's house. It would not have been a big deal, but it would have meant everything.

 

It was a lovely thought, but it was all a dream.

 

Ilya was sadly awake, and this was a nightmare.

 

He took another long sip of the drink he had been holding while daydreaming.

 

I should still go to the party. I should show my face and play the part of the Captain. I just can't, he realized.

 

The bone-deep exhaustion he sometimes felt was pulling him under. He knew there was no way he could muster the excitement to go. If he did, he felt like everyone would be able to see through him—they would see how upset he was, and that his heart was breaking. They were good people; they would ask questions. It was too much to bear. 

 

Things had been too hard lately.

 

Keeping his mental health issues from the team had been hard, but tonight it seemed absolutely impossible.

 

Maybe if you actually told him everything, Shane would have reacted differently. Maybe you’ve been bottling up too much? Maybe just telling Galina things isn’t enough, he thought.

 

He had to tell Bood he was not going to the fucking party. He didn’t want to concern his teammates by not showing up with no explanation. It would raise more questions than he was willing to answer.

 

He took another long, slow sip of his drink and slipped into the persona the public saw: the enigmatic and gregarious Ilya. It was the mask that protected and served him fairly well most of his life. There were times looking back, when he wished he had let that mask slip a little with Shane. Maybe they would’ve gotten their act together a little sooner, but that was neither here nor there.

 

Quickly, he texted Bood.

 

Ilya: Sorry. Can’t make it. Have fun tonight. Don't do anything I wouldn't do 😈 

 

After sending the text, he let his phone fall to the far side of the couch. It landed face down, lodged between the cushions. He could see it, but he didn’t care enough to move to pick it up.

 

It was the kind of text that kept up appearances even though it was a lie, and his heart was breaking.

 

Part of him hated that he was not the one hosting parties like he used to in Boston. Halloween was nice, and Shane came after… but other than that, he had not done much in the way of team bonding. Every spare minute that wasn’t focused on hockey was spent travelling to or waiting for Shane.

 

Slowly sipping his vodka, he contemplated every bad decision he’d ever made, every hurtful thing he’d ever said to Shane Hollander and vice versa.

 

Eventually, his glass was empty, and he was hungry. He went into the kitchen and heated some of the leftovers that David had given him and Shane. He left the things he knew Shane would be willing to eat in the fridge—just in case. Ilya knew their schedules were busy, and this was the last time they would see each other for at least a week, but he could not bear the thought of not leaving it for Shane. If he had to throw it out in a few days; it was wasteful, but better for his mental health.

Then he waited to see which would happen first: Ilya’s call or the food’s arrival.
